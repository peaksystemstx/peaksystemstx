The research

Why regular solar panel care pays

You don't have to take our word for it. Peer-reviewed studies, a multi-year utility-scale case study, and U.S. Department of Energy research all reach the same conclusion: dirty panels cost real money, and cleaning them pays for itself.

1. Maximizing energy output

Soiling acts as a physical barrier that prevents sunlight from reaching the photovoltaic cells. While light dust might only cause minor losses, heavy accumulation can cripple a system.

The evidence: A 2025 peer-reviewed study published in the journal Energies evaluated solar performance in a dry climate. The researchers found that typical soiling caused an 18.8% loss in power compared to a panel cleaned daily. They further noted that severe dust accumulation — just 4 grams per square meter — can slash a solar module's output power by up to 40%.

A Study on the Performance of Soiled Solar Photovoltaic Panels (Energies, MDPI) →

2. High return on investment

For homeowners and utility-scale solar farms alike, the cost of cleaning is often entirely offset by the value of the "recovered" electricity that would otherwise have been lost to dirt.

The evidence: A multi-year case study by the Institute for Decarbonization and Energy Advancement (University of Kentucky) at the E.W. Brown Solar Facility found a 6.3% performance drop attributed strictly to soiling. The study concluded that as long as washing costs stay efficient (under $0.42 per panel in their large-scale model), the cost of cleaning is fully recovered in a year or less through the resulting energy gain.

Case Study Report: Solar PV Panel Performance and Soiling Analyses (University of Kentucky) →

3. Government and industry backing

The U.S. Department of Energy and the National Renewable Energy Laboratory recognize soiling as such a significant hurdle to grid efficiency that they actively fund technology designed to optimize cleaning.

The evidence: In Round 7 of the American-Made Solar Prize, the DOE awarded competition funding to teams like Solar Unsoiled, which develops predictive software that helps large solar farms optimize their cleaning schedules. The DOE notes these cleaning innovations are vital to "increase system energy yield and reduce panel maintenance costs."

American-Made Solar Prize Round 7 (U.S. Department of Energy) →

4. Preventing long-term hardware damage

If localized soiling — a baked-on bird dropping, a cluster of wet leaves — is left in place, it can create "hot spots." The shaded cells can't generate power, so they begin acting as a resistor against the power generated by the clean cells around them. The dirty cell heats up significantly, which degrades panel materials and can permanently shorten the lifespan of the system.

The evidence: A study published in Universidad de Antioquia's engineering journal, Revista Facultad de Ingeniería, found that even light accumulation causes up to a 6% efficiency reduction during peak solar radiation. Leaving accumulation to graduate from "light" to "heavy" worsens transmission losses and localized stress.

Analysis of the soiling effect on solar-panel power efficiency (Redalyc) →

The bottom line

Does your system need it?

If you live in a climate with frequent, heavy rain, your panels may self-clean enough to avoid severe losses. But in arid regions, areas prone to wildfire ash, or near agricultural dust or heavy traffic — conditions familiar to much of Central Texas — regular professional cleaning is a financially sound maintenance requirement that pays for itself in recovered electricity.
